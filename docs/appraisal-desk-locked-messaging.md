# Appraisal Desk: Locked Messaging

**Status:** Drafted July 2026. This is the single source of truth for every headline, claim, pitch, email, article, and page we publish. It replaces the Locked Messaging Guide v2 and the messaging foundation doc, and it absorbs the decisions from both. The website gets built FROM this doc. If any other doc or page disagrees with this one, this one wins.

**How to use it:** the locked key lines are used as written. Everything else is direction: say it in these terms, in this order, with these numbers. Before anything ships, run it through The Filter at the bottom.

**The standing rule underneath everything:** every public claim is a literal engineering contract. If the platform doesn't do it yet, we don't say it yet. When it ships, the claim ships.

## 1. What We Sell

Amazon sells convenience. Shopify sells independence. Appraisal Desk sells efficiency.

Concretely, we sell a number going up: more orders handled per person. "Do more without adding people." Every feature, every headline, and every page has to trace back to that number. The money is how we get the meeting. The proof is how we win the deal. Efficiency is what the customer actually buys.

We do not sell appraisal order management software. Almost nobody wakes up shopping for that category, and leading with it shrinks our market to people who already know it exists. There's a well-known idea in product marketing: a saddle company can sell saddles, or it can sell horseback riding. We're selling the riding. The market we own is lenders, credit unions, and AMCs running their own appraisal desk instead of renting one from an AMC. No competitor is standing on that ground, and that's the point.

**Who we want the customer to become:**
- A credit union that runs its own appraisal process and keeps the margin it used to hand to an AMC.
- A desk team that gets the busywork off its plate and handles far more orders without hiring.

Those are the two things that drive the decision: busywork gone (the operator's win) and margin kept (the executive's win). Lead with both, everywhere. Other true benefits, like reporting that finally proves the department's value, are worth listing. They are support. They never carry a headline.

Treat the clarifier for what it is: it clears the runway so the real pitch can land. At least five competitors say "we're not an AMC" almost word for word, so it removes confusion but it never wins the deal, and it's never the headline.

## 3. The Math

**The price is public: $55 per order. Flat. No setup fee, no monthly minimum, nothing else.**

We show the price on purpose. A prospect can subtract our cost at any time, and that's fine. Honesty is the point, not hiding the number.

**The savings story, in one breath:** a typical AMC charges about $150 an order to manage it. Appraisal Desk runs the same work for $55. The $95 difference is the customer's, on every order. At 50 orders a week, that's about $247,000 a year kept in the building.

**Lead with what they keep.** The kept number ($95 an order, $247K a year) is the headline. The gross number ($390K a year walking out the door at that volume) works as the attention-getter, but the promise is always the difference, never the gross.

**The member tie (credit unions).** The savings story never stops at the balance sheet. A credit union is member-owned, so every dollar kept is a dollar working for members: better rates, more lending capacity, more service. When the audience is a credit union, close the money story with the member benefit, not just the number.

**We don't bad-mouth AMCs, ever.** Many AMCs run good operations, including our anchor customer. The pitch is always what the customer gains by owning the desk, never what's wrong with their AMC. We describe old ways plainly (email billing, spreadsheets, manual panels) without attacking anyone, and we highlight benefits instead of assigning blame.

**The honesty rules on the math:**
- The $150 is the AMC's management fee. The appraiser gets paid their fee either way. What changes is who keeps the management margin.
- Never imply the desk runs itself for free. Their team does the work, and the pitch is "scale without adding headcount," not "zero operating cost."
- Never imply the appraisal itself gets cheaper. The savings is the middleman fee, and we say so plainly.
- $390K is gross spend, $247K is what they keep after paying us. Don't mix them up, and don't let a headline mix them up either.

(Internal reference only, never in public copy: a known competitor publishes $99 per order. We're lower and we say our number proudly. We do not say theirs.)

## 4. The Core Promise: Do More Without Adding People

This is the promise that ties everything together and it speaks to every person in the room.

**The promise, plain:** your team handles more orders without adding people. Volume climbs, payroll doesn't have to.

**How we prove it (the receipts).** We don't have a headline stat yet, so we prove the promise by mechanism: name every manual job the platform eliminates and let the reader do the math on what their team gets back.

- Nobody makes status calls. The platform sends automatic updates to everyone who's waiting on the order.
- Nobody babysits deadlines. The platform watches every active file and flags the one that's about to slip before it slips: an appraiser who hasn't accepted, a report approaching its due date, a gap that's about to become a delay.
- Nobody renames PDFs. Incoming documents are read, renamed, and filed automatically.
- Nobody chases payments or reconciles money in a spreadsheet. Every charge, adjustment, and payout is tracked in the platform, start to finish.
- Nobody builds a panel by hand or manages rotation manually. Assignment runs automatically by grade and geography.
- Nobody runs a first-pass QC checklist by eye. The platform checks every report before a human opens it.

When the busywork disappears, the same team does more. That's the entire mechanism, and it's why the promise is safe to make.

**The protection: simpler to use, not simpler in what it does.** Full compliance, full financial trail, full capability set, UAD 3.6 in the workflow. We took the friction out, not the capability. A switcher's biggest fear is losing something she relies on, so "simpler" never reads as "fewer features."

**The other protection: this is not a layoff pitch.** "Without adding people" means growth without hiring, not cuts. The routine gets automated. The judgment stays human. The team stops doing robot work and starts doing the work that actually needs a person. Say it that way, especially to operators. Our anchor customer is living proof: they use the freed-up time to out-serve their competitors. Our customer can use it to keep margin, grow volume, or both.

**Never say "operational efficiency" or its cousins ("streamlined," "optimize your workflow").** Nobody feels a buzzword. Say what it means:
- The busywork is gone.
- Handle more without hiring.
- More orders per person.
- The day-to-day stops eating your team.
- You grow the volume, not the headcount.

## 6. Who It's For

Credit unions are the lead audience. Lenders and AMCs are real customers too and nothing on the site should exclude them, but the credit union in-house story is the wedge we lead with.

### The Three Credit Union Buyers

**1. Fully outsourced.** No desk at all, everything goes to an AMC. Pitch the business case from scratch: the math, the pre-built panel, the built-in quality and compliance, and the honest note that they'll need a person or two running the desk (which the savings funds many times over).

**2. Desk but still outsourcing (the sweet spot).** They have the team and the process, and they still route orders through an AMC. The "we'd have to build a department" objection is already dead: the people exist. The only change is the software and who keeps the margin. This buyer gets our best energy.

**3. Desk on competitor software (the switcher).** Her fears: volume goes up and she has to beg for headcount, volume goes down and someone questions why the department exists, and she has no data to prove her team's value. She is not shopping. The pitch is never "we're newer." It's: your team handles more without adding people, here's exactly how, everything you do today happens here too in one system, and since UAD 3.6 forces a change anyway, move once. What she needs to see to switch with confidence: the complete capability list (so nothing she relies on is missing), honest LOS integration status, a low-risk operator-led migration, and the move-once UAD 3.6 story.

### Reaching Credit Unions

Decisions happen at the COO or CEO level, with the day-to-day champion in VP of Lending or mortgage operations. We reach them through their leagues (the state and regional associations credit unions belong to). A league rep doesn't buy the software, but sits in front of dozens or hundreds of members, so one relationship opens many doors. The league message is member-first: your members' credit unions are paying AMCs six figures a year for a process they could run in-house, and we're the proven, independent software that lets them do it, with UAD 3.6 already handled.

### Credit Union Language Rules

- Members, not borrowers, whenever we're talking about the credit union's people. Member experience, not customer experience. They notice.
- Speak to the examiner reality without being asked. Compliance isn't an objection to handle later, it's a first-class message (see Differentiator 6).

### Ideal Volume

- Minimum: 10 orders per week
- Sweet spot: 50 to 500 orders per week
- Team size: 1 to 20 people

Don't position for enterprise AMCs. "Already running in-house on a competitor" is NOT excluded; that's the switcher and we welcome her.

## 7. UAD 3.6

**The claim, verified true:** Appraisal Desk was built for UAD 3.6 from day one. Not retrofitted, not "being updated." Built for it.

**The distinct claim nobody else can match:** everyone else built UAD 3.6 for their core user and stopped there. We built the whole workflow around it, for everyone who touches the order: the desk running it, the lenders ordering it, and the appraisers completing it. UAD 3.6 turns "pick a form" into "answer questions about the property," and our platform generates the right product from those answers, prices it automatically, and flows every order detail to the appraiser so they can scope the assignment accurately before accepting. Legacy presets still let a user "order a 1004" while the system handles the new format underneath. When every user's experience works, orders move faster, and that's the whole reason a team can do more without adding people.

**The forcing function:** the mandate lands November 2026. Every credit union and lender has to change something this year. If your platform (or your AMC's platform) is scrambling to retrofit, you'll live through that scramble. Switch to the platform that was built for it, and you make the transition once.

**One tense rule, everywhere:** the software is ready (present tense, done). What's in motion is onboarding and panel readiness (theirs, not ours). Never phrase anything so the software sounds unfinished.

**Never say "only" about UAD 3.6 readiness.** Every competitor is claiming 3.6 readiness right now, and "only" invites a fact-check we don't need. The comparative version (everyone else built it for their core user, we built it for everyone) makes the same point and nobody can dispute it with a screenshot.

## 8. The Differentiators, Ranked

No single one is unique on its own. The moat is the combination, and the combination ladders up to one promise: you do more, with less friction, and you keep the margin. Each differentiator has to resolve to that outcome, never stop at the feature.

### 1. Proven in a Live, High-Volume AMC

Our anchor customer is an AMC that has trusted this platform with 20,000 appraisals a year, 400+ orders a week, for 15 years. The platform was hardened in a real, high-demand operation long before it was sold as software. It doesn't learn on your orders.

And here's the part money can't buy: our anchor customer's own website describes what the platform does for them, in production, today. Automated assignment, proactive flagging, automated status updates, first-pass quality checks. We didn't write their copy. Their site is a standing testimonial, and we should treat it that way: "don't take our word for it, look at how the AMC that runs on us describes their operation."

Framing rules: we are software people first. Home Base is our anchor customer, not our parent and not our origin story as an AMC spinout. Use the volume numbers. Never make it sound like they're the only customer.

### 2. The Panel Comes With It

The scariest question for anyone leaving an AMC is "where do our appraisers come from?" Our answer: you're not starting from zero. Thousands of vetted appraisers are already on the platform, across all 50 states, and automated rotation puts your orders in front of qualified local appraisers the day you go live. No panel-building project. No begging appraisers to join one more portal. The network effect works both directions: appraisers sign up once and get work everywhere, which means every desk on the platform gets a deeper bench.

### 3. A Complete Back Office That Stays in One System

Not "the only platform that tracks the money" (competitors run full trails too). What we own: it all stays in one system, proven at volume, and nothing exits to a spreadsheet or a separate accounting tool.

- Collects payment from members and lenders (each order configurable for who pays, in what split)
- Holds funds during the order lifecycle
- Pays out the appraiser and tracks every fee
- Tracks every price change: who, when, how much
- Issues refunds, manages credit card fees, generates financial reports, issues 1099s
- Every party logs in and sees exactly what was charged, adjusted, and paid

The outcome: no chasing payments, no reconciling outside the platform, hours back every week. That's the do-more promise in action.

Caveat that stays off the site: appraiser payment speed depends on lender cooperation. Say "paid fast when the lender approves," never "instant."

### 4. Built for UAD 3.6, for Every User

See Section 7. In the ranked stack, this is both a differentiator and the reason to act now.

### 5. Quality Is Built Into the Platform

The unspoken objection to leaving an AMC: "who catches the bad reports if we run this ourselves?" The answer is the platform.

- Every submitted report runs through automated checks before a human opens it: completeness, data consistency, photo requirements, compliance flags.
- Every appraiser is graded on every completed report, and grades drive assignment priority. Good work earns the next order. Weak work gets fewer chances until it improves.
- Rotation by grade and geography means quality is enforced by the system, not by a manager's memory.

The QC muscle a credit union pays its AMC for is built into the software. Say it that plainly.

### 6. Compliance You Can Show Your Examiner

For a credit union, the first filter on any vendor is "will this survive our exam?" So we answer it before they ask, in our voice: nothing here is a binder we assembled for the audit. It's just how the platform works.

- Every order, every dollar, every document, every change: logged automatically, timestamped, attributable. The trail exists before anyone asks for it.
- Appraiser independence requirements are enforced in the workflow, not by policy memo.
- SOC 2 audited (SSAE 18), PCI compliant.
- Vendor due diligence answered honestly and fast: who we are, how the platform is run, what happens to their data.

### 7. A Team That Builds What You Need

Ask our anchor customer why the platform fits their operation so well: because for 15 years, when their desk needed something, we built it. That's the deal we offer every customer. If your desk truly needs something the platform doesn't do, tell us, and if it's real, we'll build it. We're a small team, we ship fast, and when you reach out you get a real answer: an immediate AI-drafted response that either resolves it or tells you plainly that a human is one reply away, and the human actually replies.

What we never claim: "24/7 support," "dedicated account manager," or anything implying a big support org. The differentiator is the culture. When you reach out, you'll feel like you matter, and that's the thing a customer who loves you says about a small company that a big competitor can't buy.

## 9. AI: What We Say

The principle: AI is claimed on specific features solving specific problems, with the problem named. Never generalized. "AI-powered platform" says nothing. "AI reads and files every incoming document so nobody renames PDFs" says everything.

**The four AI claims we make (and the only four):**
- **Document handling.** Incoming documents are read, renamed, and filed automatically. Nobody renames PDFs by hand. It's one of the most tedious daily tasks on any desk, and it's gone.
- **Automated quality checks.** Every submitted report gets a first pass before a human opens it: completeness, data consistency, photos, compliance flags.
- **AI quality review.** Submitted documents are compared against order data, and discrepancies a checklist can't catch get flagged. Manual quality review is one of the biggest time costs in this industry, and we're eliminating it.
- **AI-assisted assignment.** Rotation runs by grade and geography, and the AI layer keeps getting smarter about putting the right order in front of the right appraiser.

**Why we claim all four now:** quality review and assisted assignment are shipping before any customer outside our anchor touches the platform. By the time a prospect sees a demo, they're live. That's the deal: the claim ships because engineering ships. If anyone asks to see one of these before it's fully live, we're straight about exactly where it stands. We never fake a demo.

**The line we hold:** these four, and nothing else. A new AI capability earns its claim by shipping, or by being on the ship-before-sales track with an owner and a date. Never by sounding good in copy.

## 12. Voice

**The personality:** happy, helpful, plainspoken, confident without bravado. We sound like people who have done this job and will pick up the phone. Casual but respectful. Plain language, and if jargon is ever necessary, we explain it in the same breath.

**The one principle that carries "we're tech-first": show the receipts.** We never say "we're a technology company," "tech-forward," or "leveraging AI to modernize your workflow." Tech confidence comes through in how we describe problems: name what's broken, say exactly what we built, say what it means for the reader. The confidence lives in the specificity.

- Industry voice: "We leverage AI and automation to modernize your appraisal workflow."
- Our voice: "Email billing still exists in this industry. We don't do it. Every charge, every adjustment, every payout is tracked in the platform automatically."

**The three techniques:**

1. **Name the old way, then describe our way.** The old way isn't bad, it's just old. "Most platforms send an invoice and wait. We collect payment upfront, hold it, and pay everyone out automatically when the order closes."
2. **Be specific about what "automated" means.** Not "automated compliance tracking" but "every action is logged automatically; you'll have a full audit trail before anyone asks for one."
3. **Frame the roadmap as direction, not features.** "We're building property data auto-fill so orders start prefilled instead of blank. Retyping information that already exists is exactly the kind of work we eliminate." That signals we understand the problem, we're actively building, and the platform is on a trajectory. Tech-company energy, zero buzzwords.

**What the site should never sound like:** corporate ("enterprise-grade solutions"), desperate ("the only platform you'll ever need"), vague ("intelligent, automated, seamless"), or like a legacy company that added a website ("decades of experience for your appraisal needs").

**Hard rules:**
- Never use em dashes. Anywhere. Periods, commas, colons, or parentheses.
- Never abbreviate the company to two letters. Always spell out Appraisal Desk.
- Never generalize AI. We claim AI on specific features solving specific problems (Section 9 is the whole list). Never toss "AI-powered" onto anything for the sake of having AI in the sentence.
- Never bad-mouth AMCs or anyone else. Highlight what the customer gains, never what somebody else does wrong.
- Never run the "AMCs underpay appraisers" line.
- No competitor names in public copy.
- Never frame us as an AMC or an AMC spinout. We are a software company with an AMC anchor customer.
- In credit union copy: members, not borrowers.
- Mild profanity is fine in opinion pieces where it's genuinely us. It's rarely necessary.

**The gut check for every sentence:** would Dal say this in a first conversation with a prospect? If not, rewrite it.

## 16. The Filter: Run Every Line Through This

1. **Outcome, not feature.** Does this sell something the reader already wants, or does it stop at describing the software? If it stops at the feature, rewrite it into the result.
2. **Our ground, not a fight we'll lose.** Does this defend our market (run your own desk, do more, keep the margin), or does it pick a fight that dies on a prospect's first search ("we're the only X")? Stay on our ground.
3. **Translated, and true.** Is it aimed at a specific reader (the operator who feels the busywork, or the executive who sees the dollars), and does the product literally deliver what the line promises today?

And the gut check over all three: would Dal say this in a first conversation with a prospect? If not, it isn't ready.
