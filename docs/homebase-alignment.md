# How the Home Base Site Should Refer to Appraisal Desk
### An AI prompt for updating homebaseamc.net

**How to use this doc:** paste this entire document into your AI tool, followed by the copy of the page you're editing, and ask it to apply these rules. It works page by page. You can also just read it and make the edits by hand. Either way, the rules are the same.

## Context (read this first)

Appraisal Desk is an independent software company. Home Base is its anchor customer: 15 years running on the platform, 20,000 appraisals a year, 400+ orders a week. Appraisal Desk is now sold as standalone software to lenders, credit unions, and AMCs.

Here's why the wording matters. Appraisal Desk's biggest sales obstacle is that prospects assume it's an AMC tool, owned by an AMC, with an AMC's conflicts of interest. When the Home Base site calls the platform "proprietary" or "our custom platform," it confirms that wrong assumption for anyone researching Appraisal Desk. And prospects will research. The Home Base site is the first thing they find.

The goal is not to hide the relationship. The relationship is the proof. The goal is to describe it accurately: Home Base runs on Appraisal Desk, works closely with the team that builds it, and gets a platform that fits like it was built custom, because a lot of it was built from Home Base's direct feedback. That's a partnership story, and it's a better story than ownership anyway.

---

## The One Rule

**Appraisal Desk is software Home Base uses and helped shape. It is not software Home Base owns or built.**

Every edit below follows from that sentence. When in doubt, ask: does this phrasing make a reader think Home Base owns the platform? If yes, fix it.

---

## Naming Rules

1. The product name is **Appraisal Desk**. Two words. Capital A, capital D. Always.
2. Never write "AppraisalDesk" (one word).
3. Never write "Home Base AppraisalDesk" or "Home Base Appraisal Desk" as a product name.
4. Never abbreviate to "AD."
5. On first mention on a page, introduce it with context: "Appraisal Desk, the platform we run on" or "our technology platform, Appraisal Desk." After that, just "Appraisal Desk" or "the platform."
6. "Powered by Appraisal Desk" is always a safe construction.

## Find and Replace

| Current phrasing | Replace with |
|---|---|
| "Proprietary Technology" (section label) | "Our Technology" or "The Platform We Run On" |
| "our proprietary AI-powered platform" | "Appraisal Desk, the platform we run our entire operation on" |
| "Home Base AppraisalDesk" | "Appraisal Desk" |
| "AppraisalDesk" (anywhere, one word) | "Appraisal Desk" |
| "our custom platform" | "Appraisal Desk, built in close partnership with our team" |
| "our Appraisal Desk platform" | "the Appraisal Desk platform" or "Appraisal Desk" |
| "Meet Home Base Appraisal Desk" | "Powered by Appraisal Desk" or "The Platform Behind Our Service" |

The pattern: remove possessives and ownership language around the product name. "Our platform" is fine in casual references where the name isn't attached (every company says "our platform" about software they license). The problem is pairing ownership words directly with the Appraisal Desk name, or using words like "proprietary" and "custom-built" that literally claim ownership.

## The Relationship Story (approved language)

Use this block, or something close to it, wherever the site explains the technology relationship. Adjust to fit the page, but keep the facts and the frame:

> "Home Base runs entirely on Appraisal Desk, independent appraisal desk software we've used in production for 15 years. We work directly with the team that builds it, and when our operation needs something, they build it. That's why the platform fits our workflow like it was made for it: a lot of it was shaped by our feedback, order by order, for over a decade."

Why this works for both companies:
- Home Base still gets full credit for a platform advantage competitors can't match. "Shaped by 15 years of our feedback" is arguably stronger than "proprietary," because proprietary just means owned, while this means battle-tested.
- Appraisal Desk gets an accurate public record: independent vendor, deeply responsive, anchor customer proving it at volume.
- It's all true.

## UAD 3.6: Fix the Tense

The UAD 3.6 Resource Hub currently says things like the platform's automated QC "is being updated" and the API layer "is being updated" for the new format. That framing is backwards and it undercuts both companies. Appraisal Desk was built for UAD 3.6 and is ready now. What's in transition is Home Base's operation: moving lender clients onto the platform and getting the appraiser panel ready for the new requirements.

Rewrite any "the platform is being updated for 3.6" language along these lines:

> "Appraisal Desk was built for UAD 3.6 from day one, so the platform side of this transition is already done. Our work is making sure every client and every appraiser on our panel is ready before November, and we're doing that now."

Rule of thumb: the software is ready (present tense, done). The people and panel readiness work is ongoing (present progressive, in motion). Never phrase it so the software sounds unfinished.

## Describing Platform Capabilities (recommended)

Not required, but strongly recommended for consistency with how Appraisal Desk describes itself publicly. Both sites will be read by the same prospects, and they should tell the same story.

- Avoid blanket "AI-powered" labels. Name the specific thing instead. "AI reads and files incoming documents automatically" beats "AI-powered platform" every time.
- Describe automation by what it removes: no status calls, no chasing appraisers, no renaming PDFs, no reconciling in spreadsheets.
- Capabilities that are live get present tense. Capabilities in development get "coming" or "being built," clearly separated from what's live today.

- Testimonials, badges, and trust content.
